About

I usually start by asking how the pieces fit together.

I'm a software engineer working mostly on backend services, integrations, and automation. A lot of my work involves changes that cross several services, components, or teams.

I’m a software engineer working primarily on backend and product systems. My experience spans enterprise product R&D, platform and integration work, financial data pipelines, applied ML, and technical instruction. I tend to be most useful when a problem crosses service or team boundaries and somebody needs to make the system understandable before changing it.

Changes rarely stay inside one ticket. Dependencies, ownership boundaries, and the next recurrence of a problem all need to be visible before the change is safe.

Tests, runbooks, diagrams, and explanations turn that context into something the next person can inspect and use.

In teaching and collaboration, difficult technical problems become diagrams, examples, and trade-off discussions that people can question and reuse.

Principle

Separate what changes from what stays the same

Make the differences that affect behavior visible instead of burying them in special cases.

Principle

Make the next change easier

Use tests, automation, and documented steps so recurring work does not need to be rediscovered.

Principle

Keep ownership clear

A good abstraction should make it clearer who owns what and where a decision belongs.
