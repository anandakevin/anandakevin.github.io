About

The work taught me to make boundaries visible.

I'm a software engineer working mostly on backend services, integrations, and automation. A lot of my work involves changes that cross several services, components, or teams.

I’m a software engineer working primarily on backend and product systems. My path has moved from financial data pipelines and internal tooling to platform and integration work, enterprise product R&D, and applied ML and mobile systems.

That range changed how I work. In a ledger system, correctness and recoverability mattered more than a fast migration. In integration work, the hard part was often making environment differences and service ownership visible. In product R&D, the same discipline appears as tracing a rule through validation, configuration, and apply paths before changing it.

Teaching is connected to that engineering work rather than separate from it. Explaining APIs, pipelines, or project architecture forces me to find the model that makes the system understandable to somebody else. The resulting diagram, test, runbook, or example is useful because it can be questioned and reused.

I still start with the concrete problem, the boundary where it can fail, and the evidence that would tell us the change worked.

Principle

Correctness comes before speed

Financial and operational systems made the cost of an unverified shortcut impossible to ignore.

Principle

Boundaries become visible under change

Integration and product work taught me to trace a rule through the services that depend on it.

Principle

Explanation is part of the work

Teaching reinforces the same habit: turn a complicated system into a model people can inspect and question.
